<p><strong>TL;DR:</strong> Most tile failures trace back to subfloor prep, not the tile itself: excess deflection (floor flex under load) and out-of-tolerance flatness (generally 1/8" over 10 feet, tighter for large-format tile) are the two biggest causes of cracked tile and grout. Tile also needs backer board, an uncoupling membrane, or another approved substrate — never bare plywood.</p>

<p>A <a href="/st-augustine/services/floor-tile-installation">tile floor installation</a> succeeds or fails largely before the first tile is ever set. Here's what actually needs to happen at the subfloor stage, and why skipping it is the single most common cause of tile problems down the line.</p>

<h2>Flatness tolerance</h2>
<p>Standard tile flatness tolerance is generally 1/8 inch of variation within a 10-foot span. Large-format tile is far less forgiving — many manufacturers and installers hold to 1/16 inch within 24 inches, since a larger tile has less flex to absorb an uneven substrate. Any subfloor outside tolerance needs self-leveling compound applied before tile installation begins; skipping this step is a leading cause of the "lippage" (uneven tile edges you can feel with a bare foot) that shows up soon after installation.</p>

<h2>Underlayment options</h2>
<p>Bare plywood or OSB subfloor should never have tile set directly on top of it. The two standard approaches are cement backer board (adds rigidity, needs modified thinset) or an uncoupling membrane like Schluter-DITRA (thinner, manages movement rather than resisting it, uses unmodified thinset). See our full <a href="/blog/backer-board-guide-cement-board-vs-ditra">cement board vs. Ditra comparison</a> for which fits a given project.</p>

<span data-media-marker="2" style="display:none"></span>

<h2>Deflection: the less visible risk</h2>
<p>Deflection — how much a floor structure flexes under weight — is invisible during a walkthrough but shows up as cracked tile or grout lines months or years later if it's not accounted for. Older Florida homes with longer joist spans or undersized framing are more prone to this, which is part of why a proper subfloor assessment matters more than it might seem for a straightforward-looking floor tile job.</p>
