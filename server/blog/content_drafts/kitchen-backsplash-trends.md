<h3>1. Large-format porcelain slabs</h3>
<p>For an ultra-modern, clean look, large-format porcelain slabs are rapidly growing in popularity. Instead of individual tiles with grout lines, a single continuous porcelain slab is custom-cut to fit the wall space between your countertop and upper cabinets. These slabs mimic the veining of natural marble like Calacatta or Carrara, but with more durability. With virtually zero grout lines, cleanup is a single wipe — practical for anyone who actually cooks in the kitchen.</p>

<h3>2. Glazed Zellige and textured tiles</h3>
<p>If you want warmth and character over stark modernism, Zellige tile is a strong choice. Originally handcrafted in Morocco, Zellige tiles are glazed terracotta tiles known for slight variation in tone, thickness, and texture. Installed, these imperfections catch and refract Florida's coastal light, creating a soft, shimmering effect. Because each tile is unique, the backsplash feels hand-crafted rather than mass-produced.</p>

<span data-media-marker="2" style="display:none"></span>

<h3>3. Stacked subway tiles</h3>
<p>Subway tile never really goes out of style, but the way it's installed keeps evolving. Instead of the traditional running-bond (brick) pattern, modern designs stack subway tiles vertically or horizontally. Stacking vertically draws the eye upward, making kitchens with standard 8-foot ceilings feel taller. A subway tile with a handmade wave texture or a soft pastel color adds depth to this classic layout.</p>

<h3>4. Geometric & hexagonal patterns</h3>
<p>Geometric tiles — elongated hexagons, pickets, and triangles — create a playful, contemporary focal point. A contrasting grout color, like soft gray against white picket tiles, highlights the geometric lines and turns the backsplash into a piece of graphic art against flat-panel cabinets.</p>

<span data-media-marker="3" style="display:none"></span>

<h3>5. Classic hand-painted ceramics</h3>
<p>Particularly popular in St. Augustine, hand-painted Spanish or Mediterranean-style ceramic tiles bring old-world character into a modern kitchen. These tiles feature intricate patterns and saturated colors like cobalt blue, terracotta, and ochre, and pair well with natural wood open shelving and warm stone countertops.</p>

<h2>Why professional backsplash installation matters</h2>
<p>Backsplashes are small compared to full bathroom remodels, but they demand precision. Outlets have to be cut out cleanly, tiles have to sit flush without lippage, and the seam between countertop and wall needs flexible silicone caulk instead of rigid grout to prevent cracking.
