The best-looking outdoor kitchens also carry the same tile across the surrounding patio or pool deck instead of treating the kitchen as a separate zone.</p>

<h2>Porcelain slab countertops</h2>
<p>For an outdoor kitchen countertop, porcelain slab has largely replaced granite as the default premium choice. It's non-porous, so it won't absorb grease or wine stains the way natural stone can without regular sealing, and it holds up to grill heat and Florida's UV exposure without fading. See our <a href="/guides/porcelain-vs-ceramic-vs-natural-stone-florida">porcelain vs. natural stone guide</a> for the fuller material comparison.</p>

<h2>Heat-rated surrounds behind the grill</h2>
<p>Any tile directly behind or beside a built-in grill needs the same heat-rating consideration as an indoor fireplace surround — porcelain, ceramic, and natural stone all qualify, but the setting mortar needs to be a high-temperature formulation, not standard thinset.</p>

<h2>Tying it into the rest of the patio</h2>
<p>The outdoor kitchens that read as a real extension of the home, rather than a separate structure bolted onto the yard, carry the same or a closely coordinated porcelain across the kitchen surround, the surrounding patio floor, and any pool deck. One continuous material story reads intentional; three different materials in one backyard reads piecemeal.</p>

<span data-media-marker="2" style="display:none"></span>

<h2>Don't skip the grout spec</h2>
<p>Standard indoor grout can fade, chalk, and crack under direct Florida sun. Outdoor installations need a UV-resistant, polymer-modified grout — see our guide on <a href="/blog/best-outdoor-patio-tile-florida-pool-decks">outdoor patio tile</a> for the full installation best practices, including expansion joints and crack-isolation membranes that apply just as much to an outdoor kitchen slab as a patio floor.</p>
