<h2>The truth about tile and grout: they are not waterproof</h2>
<p>A common misconception is that tile and grout are waterproof. In reality, cement-based grout and even many ceramic tiles are naturally porous. When you shower, water absorbs into the grout joints and slowly works its way behind the tile. If the wall cavity behind the tile lacks a proper waterproofing barrier, that moisture seeps into the drywall, wood framing, or concrete slab below. Over time, this causes hidden wood rot, structural damage, and black mold.</p>

<h2>The evolution of shower waterproofing</h2>
<h3>1. The outdated way: cement board & plastic vapor barriers</h3>
<p>For decades, standard construction involved stapling plastic sheeting to the wood studs, hanging cement backer board, and tiling directly over it. The problem: every screw driven into the cement board punctures the plastic barrier, creating hundreds of tiny pathways for water. Moisture also gets trapped between the tile and the plastic, causing the cement board to crumble and grow mold over time.</p>

<span data-media-marker="2" style="display:none"></span>

<h3>2. The traditional mud bed & PVC liner</h3>
<p>This method uses a PVC liner on the subfloor, covered by a 2-to-3-inch concrete mortar bed. Water seeps through the tile and grout, saturates the entire concrete bed, and sits on the PVC liner until it exits through weep holes in the drain. That's a wet, dark environment under your shower floor that frequently breeds bacteria, mold, and sour odors.</p>

<h3>3. The modern solution: the Schluter-Kerdi bonded membrane</h3>
<p>The Schluter-Kerdi system uses a sheet-applied, bonded waterproofing membrane made of soft polyethylene, installed directly on top of the mortar bed and backer board, immediately behind the tile. Water is stopped at the surface, right under the tile layer. The concrete bed, framing studs, and walls stay completely dry, and water is directed down the drain immediately instead of pooling.</p>

<span data-media-marker="3" style="display:none"></span>

<h2>Why we choose the Schluter system for St. Augustine & Jacksonville showers</h2>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Complete water and vapor tightness.</strong> Schluter-Kerdi is impermeable to water and water vapor, which makes it ideal for high-moisture steam showers and heavy-use master bathrooms.</li>
    <li><strong>Crack isolation.</strong> The membrane is elastomeric, so it stretches slightly to accommodate minor structural settling or thermal expansion without tearing the waterproof seal.</li>
    <li><strong>Standardized drainage.</strong> The Schluter drain assembly has integrated bonding flanges that fuse with the wall membrane, creating an unbroken waterproof connection at the most vulnerable leak point in the shower.</li>
