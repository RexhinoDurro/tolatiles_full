<h2>Why this changed</h2>
<p>The Florida legislature's deregulation push (HB 735 in 2021, extended by HB 1383 in 2023, finalized by SB 1142 in 2024) removed licensing requirements for a range of specialty trades it determined didn't need state-level occupational licensing to protect public safety, including tile, painting, flooring, and cabinetry. Local governments lost the ability to require their own local specialty license for these trades as of July 1, 2025, per <a href="https://www2.myfloridalicense.com/construction-industry/hot-topics/">DBPR's official guidance</a>.</p>

<h2>The real vetting checklist now</h2>
<p>With licensing off the table, these are the signals that actually separate a reliable installer from a risky one:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Insurance.</strong> General liability insurance covers damage to your home during the project; workers' compensation protects you if someone is injured on your property. Ask to see certificates directly, not just a verbal assurance.</li>
  <li><strong>Registered business.</strong> A legitimate business will have a Florida business registration you can look up on Sunbiz.org, plus a real business address and phone number.</li>
  <li><strong>Written contract.</strong> Scope, materials, timeline, and payment schedule should all be in writing before work starts — a verbal agreement offers no recourse if something goes wrong.</li>
  <li><strong>References or portfolio.</strong> Photos of completed work and willingness to share past client contacts are a much stronger signal than a license number ever was.</li>
  <li><strong>Workmanship warranty.</strong> A contractor who stands behind their installation with a written warranty has a direct incentive to do the job right the first time.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Building permits still matter</h2>
<p>Deregulation applies to the installer's own licensing, not to building permits — structural changes, plumbing relocations, or major bathroom renovations may still require permits pulled by a licensed general or plumbing contractor, even if the tile work itself doesn't. A reputable installer will tell you upfront when a project crosses into permit territory.</p>
